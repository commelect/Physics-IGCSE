Charge, Current & Quantities

Electric current

I = Q / t

Rate of flow of charge, in amperes (A). Conventional current flows from + to − round the circuit — opposite to the direction electrons actually move.

Electric charge

Q = I × t

Measured in coulombs (C). In a metal wire, the charge carriers are free electrons.

e.m.f.

e.m.f. = W / Q

Energy the source (e.g. a cell) gives to each coulomb of charge. Unit: volt (V). It's what drives current around a complete circuit.

p.d. (voltage)

V = W / Q

Energy transferred to a component per coulomb of charge passing through it. Measured with a voltmeter connected in parallel across the component.

Resistance

R = V / I

Unit: ohm (Ω). Ammeter goes in series to measure current; voltmeter goes in parallel to measure p.d. across the component.

What changes resistance

Longer wire → more resistance. Thicker wire (larger area) → less resistance. Higher temperature → more resistance for a metal.

Circuits & Power

Series circuits

Same current at every point. The p.d.s across each component add up to the source p.d.

R = R₁ + R₂ + …

Parallel circuits

Same p.d. across every branch. The branch currents add up to the total current from the source.

1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …

Ohm's law

V = I R

Holds for a resistor at constant temperature. Its I–V graph is a straight line through the origin.

Non-ohmic components

Filament lamp: resistance rises as it heats up, so the I–V graph curves over. Diode: conducts one way only — almost no current in reverse.

Electrical power

P = VI = I²R = V²/R

Unit: watt (W) — the rate energy is transferred.

Electrical energy

E = P × t = VIt

Household bills use kilowatt-hours: energy (kWh) = power (kW) × time (h).

Magnetism & Electromagnetic Effects

Magnetic fields

Field lines run from N to S pole outside the magnet. Lines closer together mean a stronger field.

Electromagnets

A current-carrying wire has a magnetic field of concentric circles around it. Coiling it into a solenoid concentrates the field; a soft-iron core makes it stronger.

Right-hand grip rule

Thumb points in the direction of conventional current; curled fingers show the direction of the magnetic field around the wire.

Force on a conductor (motor effect)

F = B I L

A current-carrying wire in a magnetic field feels a force. Direction is given by Fleming's left-hand rule (thuMb = Motion, First finger = Field, seCond finger = Current).

Electromagnetic induction

Moving a conductor through a magnetic field — or changing the field through a coil — induces an e.m.f. Bigger e.m.f. from: faster movement, stronger field, more turns.

Lenz's law

The direction of an induced current always opposes the change that produced it.

a.c. generator

A coil rotated in a magnetic field induces an alternating e.m.f. Slip rings keep contact with the brushes and let the current reverse every half-turn.

d.c. motor

A current-carrying coil in a magnetic field turns (forces found with Fleming's left-hand rule). A split-ring commutator reverses the current every half-turn to keep the coil spinning one way.

Transformer

Vₚ/Vₛ = Nₚ/Nₛ

Changes an alternating p.d. via primary/secondary coils on a soft-iron core. More secondary turns → step-up (voltage increases); fewer → step-down. Ideally, VₚIₚ = VₛIₛ.

Electrical Safety

Fuse

A thin wire that melts and breaks the circuit if the current gets too high, protecting the wiring from overheating and fire.

Circuit breaker

Switches off automatically once current passes a set value — and unlike a fuse, it can simply be reset afterwards.

Earthing

The earth wire connects an appliance's metal casing to the ground. If a fault makes the casing live, current flows safely to earth instead of through anyone touching it — blowing the fuse or tripping the breaker.

Double insulation

An appliance cased entirely in insulating material needs no earth wire, since the casing can never become live.

Fuse rating

Chosen just above the appliance's normal operating current — common ratings are 3 A, 5 A, and 13 A.

Wire colours

Live: brown. Neutral: blue. Earth: green and yellow stripes.
