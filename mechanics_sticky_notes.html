Measurements & Motion

Measuring length & time

Use a ruler, vernier calipers, or micrometer depending on the precision needed. For a pendulum, time several oscillations and divide, to reduce timing error.

Scalars & vectors

Scalar: magnitude only — distance, speed, mass, energy. Vector: magnitude and direction — displacement, velocity, force, acceleration.

Speed, velocity & acceleration

a = (v − u) / t

Speed = distance/time. Velocity is speed in a stated direction. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity, in m/s².

Distance–time graphs

Gradient = speed. A flat section means the object is at rest; a curve means the speed is changing.

Velocity–time graphs

Gradient = acceleration. Area under the graph = distance travelled. A flat section means constant velocity.

Equations of motion

v = u + at

Also: s = ((u+v)/2)t,  s = ut + ½at²,  v² = u² + 2as. Use these only when acceleration is constant.

Forces

Effects of a force

A force can change an object's speed, its direction of travel, or its shape. Measured in newtons (N).

Resultant force & Newton's 1st law

The resultant force is the vector sum of all forces acting. If it's zero, the object stays at rest or keeps moving at constant velocity.

Newton's 2nd law

F = m a

An unbalanced (resultant) force accelerates an object in the direction of that force.

Newton's 3rd law

Every force has an equal and opposite reaction force, acting on a different object.

Mass & weight

W = m g

Mass (kg) is the amount of matter and stays constant. Weight (N) is a force and depends on gravitational field strength g.

Terminal velocity

As speed rises, air resistance rises too, until it balances weight. The resultant force becomes zero, so the object falls at a constant (terminal) speed.

Hooke's law

F = k x

Extension is proportional to the applied force, up to the limit of proportionality. k is the spring constant, in N/m.

Moments & Momentum

Moment of a force

moment = F × d

d is the perpendicular distance from the pivot to the line of action of the force. Unit: N·m.

Principle of moments

For a system in equilibrium: sum of clockwise moments = sum of anticlockwise moments, taken about the same pivot.

Centre of mass & stability

The point where an object's whole weight appears to act. A lower centre of mass and a wider base make an object more stable.

Momentum

p = m v

Unit: kg·m/s. A vector quantity, so direction matters.

Conservation of momentum

In a closed system with no external forces, total momentum before a collision or explosion equals total momentum after.

Work, Energy & Power

Work done

W = F d

Force × distance moved in the direction of the force. Work done equals energy transferred. Unit: joule (J).

GPE & kinetic energy

GPE = mgh   KE = ½mv²

Gravitational PE depends on height above a reference point; kinetic energy depends on speed squared.

Conservation of energy

Energy can't be created or destroyed — only transferred between stores or transformed from one form to another. Total energy is always conserved.

Power

P = W / t

The rate of doing work, or the rate of energy transfer. Unit: watt (W) = one joule per second.

Pressure

Pressure

p = F / A

Unit: pascal (Pa) = N/m². Spreading a force over a larger area lowers the pressure.

Pressure in a liquid

p = ρ g h

Increases with depth and with density. At a given depth, it acts equally in all directions.

Atmospheric pressure

Caused by the weight of the air above. It decreases with altitude, since there's less air above you.

Measuring pressure

A manometer measures a pressure difference from the height difference between two liquid columns.

Transmitting pressure (hydraulics)

Pressure applied to an enclosed liquid is transmitted equally throughout it. Hydraulic jacks and brakes use pistons of different areas to multiply force.
