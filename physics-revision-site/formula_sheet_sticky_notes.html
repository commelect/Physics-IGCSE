Notes marked (derived) aren't given directly in the syllabus formula list — they come from combining two or more core equations.

Mechanics

Speed & density

speed = distance / time

ρ = m / V

Acceleration

a = (v − u) / t

Equations of motion

v = u + at

Also: s = ((u+v)/2)t, s = ut + ½at², v² = u² + 2as — the last two come from combining the first two.

Newton's 2nd law & weight

F = ma

W = mg

Weight is just F = ma with a replaced by g.

Momentum & impulse (derived)

p = mv

Ft = Δ(mv)

Impulse (Ft) equals the change in momentum — from combining F = ma with a = (v−u)/t.

Moments & Hooke's law

moment = F × d

F = kx

Work, energy & power

W = Fd  GPE = mgh

KE = ½mv²  P = W/t

Falling speed (derived)

v = √(2gh)

From energy conservation, mgh = ½mv², rearranged for the speed after falling height h from rest.

Power from force & speed (derived)

P = Fv

From P = W/t and W = Fd, since d/t = v.

Thermal Physics

Specific heat capacity

E = m c Δθ

Specific latent heat

E = m L

Temperature scale

T (K) = θ (°C) + 273

The gas laws

p / T = const.  (pressure law)

pV = const.  (Boyle's law)

V / T = const.  (volume law)

Combined gas law (derived)

p₁V₁/T₁ = p₂V₂/T₂

Merges all three individual gas laws into one relationship for a fixed mass of gas.

Waves

Wave speed

v = f λ

T = 1 / f

Refractive index

n = sin i / sin r

n = c / v

Critical angle (derived)

sin C = 1 / n

From n = sin i/sin r, applied at the critical angle, where the refracted ray grazes the boundary at 90°.

Electricity & Magnetism

Current, charge & p.d.

I = Q / t

V = W / Q

Resistance

R = V / I

Series & parallel resistance

R = R₁ + R₂ + …

1/R = 1/R₁ + 1/R₂ + …

Power (two forms derived)

P = VI

P = I²R  P = V²/R

The last two come from substituting V = IR into P = VI.

Electrical energy

E = Pt = VIt

Transformer

Vₚ/Vₛ = Nₚ/Nₛ

VₚIₚ = VₛIₛ (derived)

The second comes from energy conservation — power in equals power out for an ideal transformer.

Force on a conductor

F = B I L

Nuclear Physics

Nuclide notation

ᴬZX

A = nucleon number, Z = proton number.

Half-life

fraction remaining = (½)ⁿ

n = number of half-lives elapsed.

Conservation in decay

In any nuclear equation, total nucleon number (A) and total proton number (Z) balance on both sides — that's what makes alpha/beta decay equations work, rather than a single formula.

Space Physics

Orbital speed (derived)

v = 2πr / T

From speed = distance/time, using the orbit's circumference (2πr) over the orbital period T.

Radius & period

No set formula, but worth remembering: for objects orbiting the same body, a smaller orbital radius means a faster speed and a shorter period.
