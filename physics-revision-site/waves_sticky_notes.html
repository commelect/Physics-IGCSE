General Wave Properties

Transverse vs longitudinal

Transverse: vibrations perpendicular to travel (light, water). Longitudinal: vibrations parallel to travel, with compressions and rarefactions (sound). Waves transfer energy, not matter.

Wave terms

Amplitude: max displacement from rest. Wavelength (λ): distance between corresponding points. Frequency (f): waves per second (Hz). Period (T): time for one wave.

Wave speed

v = f λ

Also, period and frequency are related by T = 1/f.

Reflection of waves

Angle of incidence = angle of reflection, both measured from the normal.

Refraction of waves

Waves change speed (and direction, if hitting the boundary at an angle) on entering a new medium. Wavelength changes; frequency stays the same.

Diffraction

Waves spread out after passing through a gap or around an obstacle — most noticeable when the gap is a similar size to the wavelength.

Reflection & Refraction

Law of reflection

Angle of incidence = angle of reflection. Incident ray, normal, and reflected ray all lie in the same plane.

Plane mirror images

Virtual, upright, laterally inverted, the same size as the object, and as far behind the mirror as the object is in front.

Refraction of light

Light bends towards the normal entering a denser medium (slowing down), and away from the normal leaving it (speeding up).

Refractive index

n = sin i / sin r

Measured going from a less dense into a denser medium.

Speed & refractive index

n = c / v

c = speed of light in a vacuum; v = speed of light in the medium.

TIR, Dispersion & Lenses

Critical angle

The angle of incidence in the denser medium at which the refracted ray travels exactly along the boundary (angle of refraction = 90°).

Total internal reflection

Happens when the angle of incidence in the denser medium exceeds the critical angle — all the light reflects back inside. Used in optical fibres.

Dispersion

A prism splits white light into a spectrum because each colour refracts by a slightly different amount — violet bends most, red least.

Converging (convex) lens

Brings parallel rays together at a focal point. Used to correct long sight and in magnifying glasses and cameras.

Diverging (concave) lens

Spreads parallel rays apart, as if from a focal point in front of the lens. Used to correct short sight.

Focal length

Distance from the lens centre to the principal focus. A more curved (fatter) lens has a shorter focal length and is more powerful.

Electromagnetic Spectrum

The spectrum, in order

Radio waves, microwaves, infrared, visible light, ultraviolet, X-rays, gamma rays — increasing frequency and energy, decreasing wavelength.

Speed

All electromagnetic waves travel at the same speed in a vacuum: 3 × 10⁸ m/s.

Uses

Radio: broadcasting. Microwaves: cooking, satellites. Infrared: remotes, thermal imaging. UV: sterilising. X-rays: medical imaging. Gamma: sterilising, cancer treatment.

Dangers

UV can damage skin and eyes. X-rays and gamma rays are ionising and can damage or mutate cells — exposure is limited with shielding and dosage limits.

Sound

Sound waves

Longitudinal waves made of compressions and rarefactions, caused by vibrations. Need a medium — sound can't travel through a vacuum.

Speed of sound

Fastest in solids, slower in liquids, slowest in gases, since closer particles pass vibrations on more quickly. About 330–340 m/s in air.

Pitch & loudness

Pitch depends on frequency — higher frequency, higher pitch. Loudness depends on amplitude — bigger amplitude, louder sound.

Hearing range

Humans typically hear from about 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz.

Echoes

Sound waves reflecting off a hard surface. Can be used to measure distance if the speed of sound and the time delay are known.

Ultrasound

Sound above human hearing range (>20,000 Hz). Used in medical scanning and industrial testing, reflecting off boundaries between materials.
